How does the site work?

A free, independent website to find water, in Tunisia and around the world, and help others find it.

What is the site for?

Find water near you (spring, fountain, public tap, majel, well), in Tunisia and anywhere in the world, and help others by adding the points you know. The site is free, with no account.

How to use it: each feature in one line

  • Untested water: the warning above the map reminds you that the water may not be safe to drink (boil or filter it if in doubt).
  • Map: the water points of Tunisia, each with the colour of its type. Tap a point for directions (Google Maps).
  • Filters: spring, fountain, public tap, majel or well.
  • “Near me”: the 5 water points closest to you, with their distance and a “go” link.
  • The whole world: search for a town anywhere in the world or tap an example (Mecca, Istanbul, Paris…); the map reads its water points live.
  • “Also search on Google Maps”: a link that opens Google Maps if you cannot find a water point.
  • Report a water point: add a spring, a fountain, a tap, a majel or a well by placing its marker on the map.
  • “It's true” or “No longer exists”: confirm a water point, or say that it has gone.
  • Majels of Djerba: their name, their condition and a link to their record and photos on the AJEM association's website.
  • “Majels and wells in Tunisia” page: how water has been collected for centuries, with explanations and reliable links.
  • “Quran & water” page: verses in Arabic with a recognised translation of their meaning (QuranEnc), and hadiths about water.
  • Our related sites: Tunisia Water Prices (bottled water) and Mosques of Tunisia, linked on the home page.
  • 21 languages: the language button in the header opens the same page in your language.
  • Share: the button in the header opens the phone's share menu (otherwise WhatsApp).
  • On your phone: add the site to the home screen like an app; pages you have already opened stay readable offline.
  • Presentation video: a short video that shows the site.
  • Your feedback: a remark or a mistake? Write to us, every message is read.

Where do the points come from?

  • Water Point Data Exchange (WPdx, CC BY 4.0 licence): over 800,000 water points surveyed in the field in 84 countries (mostly in Africa and Asia), read live for the area shown; points reported as “broken” are not shown.
  • OpenStreetMap, the free map made by its contributors: springs, drinking fountains, public taps and water points, cisterns and wells in Tunisia, re-read every 3 months by our robot (private wells and cisterns excluded).
  • The AJEM association, Fesguietna project: the majels (rainwater cisterns) of Djerba it has surveyed, with their condition; each point links to its record and photos on the AJEM website.
  • Elsewhere in the world: the map reads OpenStreetMap live for the area shown (when you zoom in on a town or tap “Near me”).
  • Visitors: anyone can report a water point. It is published automatically (about every 2 hours), marked “to be confirmed” until another visitor confirms it.

How is it checked?

  • The robot rejects an impossible position or an unknown type, removes phone numbers and links (no advertising), and limits the number of reports per phone.
  • A point reported less than 40 m from a known point of the same type counts as a confirmation (no duplicates).
  • If several visitors say “no longer exists” (more than the confirmations), the point is removed from the map.
  • Nobody checks the water quality: it is not tested and may not be safe to drink.

Your data

Your position is only used to show nearby points, inside your phone: it is sent only if you report a water point (and then only the position of that point). An anonymous browser identifier prevents the same person from voting several times. Anonymous visit statistics, no cookies.

Automatic updates

Everything happens on its own, through our robots: the points in Tunisia are re-read every 3 months, reported points are published about every 2 hours, and every change to the site is tested before it is published.

An independent site

This site is not an official service and does not depend on any organisation. Nobody checks the water quality: ask on site.
