About and method

A free, independent website to find water, in Tunisia and around the world, and help others find it.

Where do the points come from?

  • Water Point Data Exchange (WPdx, CC BY 4.0 licence): over 800,000 water points surveyed in the field in 84 countries (mostly in Africa and Asia), read live for the area shown; points reported as “broken” are not shown.
  • OpenStreetMap, the free map made by its contributors: springs, drinking fountains, public taps and water points, cisterns and wells in Tunisia, re-read every 3 months by our robot (private wells and cisterns excluded).
  • The AJEM association, Fesguietna project: the majels (rainwater cisterns) of Djerba it has surveyed, with their condition; each point links to its record and photos on the AJEM website.
  • Elsewhere in the world: the map reads OpenStreetMap live for the area shown (when you zoom in on a town or tap “Near me”).
  • Visitors: anyone can report a water point. It is published automatically (about every 2 hours), marked “to be confirmed” until another visitor confirms it.

How is it checked?

  • The robot rejects an impossible position or an unknown type, removes phone numbers and links (no advertising), and limits the number of reports per phone.
  • A point reported less than 40 m from a known point of the same type counts as a confirmation (no duplicates).
  • If several visitors say “no longer exists” (more than the confirmations), the point is removed from the map.
  • Nobody checks the water quality: it is not tested and may not be safe to drink.

Your data

Your position is only used to show nearby points, inside your phone: it is sent only if you report a water point (and then only the position of that point). An anonymous browser identifier prevents the same person from voting several times. Anonymous visit statistics, no cookies.
